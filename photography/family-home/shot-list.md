# Family-home discovery shoot — 20 shots

**The story (producer's paragraph):** a family of three — father, mother, seven-year-old
daughter — has just discovered Phideo in their bright, luxurious open-plan home (living
room flowing into kitchen and dining). The photographs all around them — framed art,
gallery walls, the fridge door, the photo album — are coming alive through their phones,
and they're sharing the excitement with each other, room to room.

**Canon that governs every frame** (design-system §5): the phone is a **live camera
window** — the print's *own* image alive inside the screen, brighter and more saturated,
the still print frozen beside it. **Orientation lock** on every print + screen pairing:
matching orientation, edges visible inside the screen, or an unmistakable zoomed detail.
**Brightness floor:** joyful, vibrant, sunlit — no dark rooms. Brand colour enters as
objects in scene (plum throw, red mug, plum ribbon), never in post. No text anywhere.

**Casting consistency:** shot 01 is the anchor — it casts the family (father: sage-green
knit; mother: cream blouse, auburn hair; daughter: mustard cardigan, curly hair) and the
home. Every other shot is generated against it as an image reference.

**Crew notes baked into each brief:** CD = the one-line message · PH = camera/lens ·
SD = set + accents · LX = light plan · GD = what the screen shows.

screen brightest point · faces unobstructed · genuine joy (no stock-photo stiffness).

## QA outcome (2026-09-17)

All 20 shots shipped after two fix rounds (shot with GPT-Image 2.5, anchor-referenced for
casting consistency; every screen-and-print pairing gated by the CD):

- **Photographer's cull** flagged 05 (phone floated beside the frame it displayed), 11
  (screen invented a kite and a third person the print doesn't have) and 15 (screen showed
  a *wider* view than the print — a camera can only match or zoom in).
- **CD gate** passed 16 first look and added 06 (phone aimed past the fridge snapshot,
  no honest orientation read) and 07 (phone displaying wall art from across the room —
  camera-geometry break), and failed the first repairs of 11 and 15 for still drifting wide.
- **Second fix round** shipped all four. Briefing lesson recorded: "tighter crop" is
  ignored by image models — brief zooms as *"the subjects appear LARGER on the screen than
  in the print, with less scenery"*, and brief geometry as *"the phone partially overlaps
  the print it is viewing, the print's edges visible around and behind the phone."*
