Design system · consolidated 15 Sep 2026

Bring photos to life.

Phideo attaches a video to a printed photo. Point a phone at the print and the video plays over it — the phone is the gate from the real world into the digital one. Every visual choice below exists to make that one moment legible.

Logo

The “o” is the app.

“phi” in purple, “de” in red, and the o replaced by the icon: a purple ring open at the top-right, a red arc closing it, a red play triangle inside. Clearspace on every side equals the height of the o. The mark is registered (settled 17 Sep 2026): the logo carries ® across the board — the manual’s logo page pre-dates it.

Purple on white

On any gradient: white only

Not accepted: stretch, skew, rotate, shadows or bevels, colour swaps, purple on grey, red on purple, any placement on a busy photo without a scrim. Print placement: upper- or lower-left. Web and email: top-left inside a lobe in the surrounding background colour that cuts into the hero with inverted (concave) corner joins — sized so the wordmark always reads as a word, while the hero stays the point.

The o is the mark — and every play button

The o stands alone as the brand mark: it is the play affordance on every video surface and the “this is a phideo” badge on prints, posters and tags. No generic ▶ triangle appears anywhere in the product, the site or communications — if it plays, it’s the o. Full colour or monotone is decided by the ground, the way every video platform decides its overlay controls: contrast first.

Full colour — white and light neutral grounds only, where both colours hold contrast: marketing pages, packaging, the printed phideo tag.

White — on brand gradients, brand colours and dark UI. Never re-introduce the two-colour split on a coloured ground.

White over media — the play button on photos and video is always monotone white (industry standard for overlay controls), with a soft shadow for separation. No containing circle.

Single colour — purple (or black) for one-colour print, engraving, embossing. Also the fallback below ~24px, where the two-colour split stops reading.

Construction never changes: ring open at the top-right, arc closing it, triangle pointing right — all three pieces recolour together, never independently. Minimum size 16px; from 16–24px use monotone. The o alone doesn’t carry the ® — that belongs to the full logo.

Icon usage — the play mark in practice

The o’s own ring is the button. It is never wrapped in a disc, circle, pill, badge or second ring — double-ringing muddies the mark and makes it read as a generic player control. Wherever something plays, the bare o sits directly on the media or gradient, monotone white, with nothing drawn around it.

✓ Do — the bare o, monotone white, straight on the media or gradient. A soft shadow (~20–40%) is the only separation allowed.

✗ Don’t — no containing disc, no outer ring, no glass badge, and never a tinted or greyed o. The extra circle competes with the o’s own ring and kills the mark.

  • Hit area ≠ glyph — grow the touch target invisibly (≥44px); the drawn icon stays bare.
  • Colour — white over media, gradients and dark UI; full colour only on white/light neutrals; single-colour purple/black for one-colour print (see the mark rules above).
  • Everything else — icons that aren’t play affordances follow the icon families: print = thin white line icons in flat red/purple/gradient circles; web = glossy violet glyphs. The circle treatment belongs to those icons — never to the o.

Imagery

Real world, still. Phone screen, alive.

The composition rule for every image: the print exists in the world — often black-and-white, faded, framed, printed — and the same scene is alive in colour inside the phone. Both visible at once. Hands, real prints, warm light; the screen brighter than everything around it.

Five ways to show it

  • Looking — people delighted at their phone; saturated, story-first.
  • Black & white → colour — the strongest device: old print, living video.
  • Demonstrate — hands, phone, print and the printed phideo tag all in frame.
  • Usage — billboards, bus stops, business cards, galleries, posters, listings.
  • AR window (web) — the phone as a window into a rounded lavender-edged panel floating on the mesh; polaroid-cloud variant scatters photo cards around it.

Do

Real hands and real prints · the same scene in both worlds · joy, surprise, “amazed” · the phideo tag/QR visible on the print · colour inside the phone, restraint outside it.

Don’t

Empty black phone screens as the hero · people staring at nothing · AI faces with visible artefacts · purple or red duotones over faces · logo dropped on a busy photo without a scrim.

Photography

The photoshoot: six ways to say it

Every shot is directed like a sentence: one message, one composition, one light, one honest expression — and the brand colours enter as objects and light inside the scene (a plum ribbon, a red pencil, a violet screen glow), never as a filter in post. The captions below sit on the plum scrim — the same treatment any type over photography gets.

The mechanic — what every image must depict

A phideo is the photograph itself, alive. Naked eye: a still print. Through the phone: the same photograph, moving — the phone is a window into the digital world, not a player for separate content. The life stays inside the print’s own frame (it never spills past the edges or cuts to another scene), and the phone’s view may be modestly zoomed in — a tighter crop of the same image — so the motion reads on the small screen. Whenever a print and a screen share the frame: same image on both, screen a slightly tighter living crop, brighter and more saturated, the print beside it unmistakably frozen. Full-bleed footage, no app chrome.

The mechanic, first-person: a steadicam POV walk-up — the camera is the viewer’s own eyes approaching the painting — and the phone is a live camera, not a player: a landscape screen matched to the landscape painting, showing a zoomed live view where the storm plays at real speed — waves crashing against the hull as the ship cuts through the rough water. The real painting never moves; it only grows in perspective as you walk. (Seedance 2.5, two passes: a static walk-up plate, then the screen edited alive.)

Reshot 2026-09-17 (GPT-Image 2.5, 2K) with the mechanic as canon · files in photography/. Every brief is written by five roles — creative director, photographer, set dresser, lighting specialist, graphic designer — and every print/screen pair passes the mechanic gate: same image on both, screen alive and brighter. Warm shots (01, 02, 04, 05) are the everyday register; 03 and 06 are the campaign register — richer and more dramatic, but never dull.

Photos under the brand — six rules

1 · The plum scrim

Type never sits raw on a photograph. A gradient rises from the edge that carries the type — transparent → rgba(43,10,52,.84) (near-black plum, not grey) — covering the bottom 55–65%. Type on it is always white; on very dark photos (06) a neutral near-black scrim at lower opacity is enough.

2 · The screen is sacred

Scrims, washes and logos never dim the phone screen — it must stay the brightest, most saturated element in the frame. Anchor the scrim to the edge away from the screen, and crop so the screen escapes it.

3 · Accents in-scene, not in post

Purple, red and violet enter as wardrobe, props and light — ribbon, pencil, mug, raincoat, screen glow — planted at the shoot. No duotones, no colour overlays on faces, no recolouring in post.

4 · Containers do the branding

Photos live inside brand geometry: 18–28px rounded corners or the squircle window, the lobe carrying the logo in the top-left, the scrim carrying the type. The photo stays honest; the brand rides on the frame around it.

5 · Never cover the point of interest

No overlay — lobe, logo, type, badge — ever sits on the subject, above all on faces or the living screen. Overlays live on quiet image only. If a layout's lobe would touch the subject, the plate is wrong (reframe or reshoot); never ship the overlap. (Client review, 2026-09-17.)

6 · Orientation lock

The screen shows what a real camera would see. Three honest reads: the phone matches the print's orientation; or the print's edges are visible inside the screen; or the view is an unmistakable zoomed-in detail. Never the whole scene recomposed to fit the screen's aspect — a portrait phone showing an entire landscape print full-bleed breaks the camera-mode illusion instantly. (Client review, 2026-09-17.)

Photo as the hero — a layered stack, not segments

A photo hero is built as layers, one on top of the other: the photograph runs full bleed under everything; the scrim, the type and the lobe sit on top of it. The photo is never sliced into bands. The wireframe fixes only the footprints of the layers above — and its real job is to hand that knowledge to the photographer before the shoot, so the composition is built around it in camera (see production-workflow.md).

Devices

The recurring moves

Ring & arc

The o icon blown up and cropped off the page edge: letterhead watermark at 10%, folder, envelope, social corners.

Lobe

Tab in the surrounding background colour carrying the logo, cutting into a gradient hero. The joins to the hero edges curve inward (concave, radius r) and the outer corner curves outward at up to lobe height − r — at that max the arcs meet tangent in one continuous S-curve. Bigger, and the arcs no longer meet. It reads as the background cutting in, not a 90° notch. Logo sized to stay legible — but the hero stays the point. Web nav and every email header.

Squircle window

The AR frame: ~40px radius, 1px white/40% stroke, soft glow. Also the “Endless Possibilities” media holder.

Glass panel

White 20–30% fill, blur ~20px, 1px white/40% border, radius 24–32, soft shadow. Captions, forms, “Our mission”.

Tracked tagline pill

Translucent dark pill, white Amenti with wide word-spacing: “Scan.  Watch.  Experience.”
